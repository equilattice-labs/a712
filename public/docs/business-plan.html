1. Executive summary

Voxaura lets people attach a direct reward to useful online work. Its entry point is a public X post: the research thread that saved an hour, the tutorial that solved a problem, or the artwork that deserved more than a like. A supporter funds a transparent escrow. The designated recipient claims before the deadline; otherwise, the supporter can request an onchain refund.

The product has two clearly separated testnet modes. The main author-rewards integration resolves a public X post to its permanent author ID, accepts native test ETH or a clearly labeled six-decimal tUSD demo token, records a public note of up to 140 Unicode characters, and escrows the reward for 90 days. A successful claim sends 97% to the creator and 3% to the disclosed fee recipient; an expired unclaimed reward is fully refundable. Real X authentication and sponsored claims require operator credentials and configuration. The independently usable wallet pilot remains available with explicit recipient wallets, zero protocol fees, and its original shorter deadlines.

The remaining identity milestone is live validation with actual X accounts. The delivered OAuth/attestation service resolves immutable author IDs, authenticates with PKCE, binds a wallet proof to the exact reward contract, and issues a short-lived claim authorization. A configured relayer can submit the claim and pay its network gas, subject to caps and replay controls. Without valid X developer credentials the author flow remains disabled; synthetic and mocked tests are not live X verification. The service introduces a trusted verifier and still needs operational hardening and independent review before commercial use.

The larger business is a rewards infrastructure layer for creators and communities: individual appreciation payments, curated creator campaigns, transparent payout reporting, and an API for communities that want to reward useful contributions. The defensible assets would be trusted creator relationships, campaign workflows, distribution integrations, reliable identity operations, and accumulated operating knowledge. A smart contract or a token alone is not a moat.

The proposed commercial model is a 3% fee on successfully claimed rewards, supplemented by paid campaign software. It does not monetize unclaimed funds or promise investment returns. A plausible early base case remains loss-making: 5,000 monthly paying supporters, four $12 rewards per supporter, an 80% claim rate, and ten $299 campaign customers produce an illustrative $8,750 monthly revenue. This supports validation, not self-sufficiency. Scaling responsibly requires repeat usage, larger aggregate reward volume, and demonstrated B2B willingness to pay.

The first funding objective is an illustrative $900,000 pre-seed round for an 18-month validation program, conditional on team formation and investor interest. No fundraising has occurred. The release gates are evidence-based: prove that rewards reach the intended recipient, creators want to claim them, supporters return without token subsidies, and the service can cover direct costs before broadening scope.

2. Problem, users, and initial market

The problem

Social platforms are good at capturing attention but offer a limited bridge between appreciation and direct payment. A reader may value a single post without wanting a subscription. A creator may not have published a wallet address or payment link. Ecosystem teams may want to reward useful educational content without negotiating individual sponsorships or relying on opaque impression metrics.

These are hypotheses about unmet needs, not a claim that existing creator-payment tools fail universally. Interviews and payment behavior must establish which friction is important enough to support a business.

The initial serviceable cohort target is 100 participating creators, with 100 reachable engaged readers each. That creates 10,000 potential exposures, not 10,000 customers. At an assumed 8% qualified-visit rate, 40% wallet connection rate, and 25% first-funding rate, the cohort yields 80 first supporters. These assumptions are intentionally modest and must be replaced by measured funnel data. They demonstrate why a small creator launch is a learning program rather than a revenue story.

Campaign buyers are a separate funnel: 40 qualified community operators interviewed, ten design partners invited, five completed pilots, and three willingness-to-pay confirmations are early targets. No organizations have agreed to participate.

鈥淲allet connected鈥?is a browser session state, not proof of an X account and not automatically a secure server login. The optional X server uses a one-time wallet challenge bound to origin, reward, account ID, and expiry; this must be tested with actual operator credentials before activation.

The wallet pilot loop

  1. A supporter chooses a public post and obtains the intended recipient's wallet address through a trusted channel.
  2. The supporter connects a wallet and checks the testnet, recipient, amount, and expiry shown before signing.
  3. The contract receives test ETH and records the escrow. The UI displays the actual escrow ID and transaction receipt.
  4. The supporter can copy the receipt link and choose to share it manually. The app does not automatically post or message anyone.
  5. The recipient connects the designated wallet and claims within the permitted period.
  6. If the reward expires unclaimed, the original payer sends a refund transaction. Expiry makes a refund available; it does not imply an automatic transfer without a transaction.
  7. The UI reflects the terminal state and links to chain evidence.

A post URL in this version is contextual metadata. It must not be interpreted as evidence that the recipient address belongs to that author. This distinction is visible in the reward form and explained in the FAQ.

The author-rewards integration loop

The delivered optional integration resolves a submitted post through the official X API and funds a separate identity escrow using the numeric post ID and author account ID. The creator authenticates using OAuth with PKCE, proves control of a payout wallet with a one-time nonce challenge, and receives a short-lived typed authorization. The contract checks the verifying signer, chain/contract domain, reward ID, recipient and deadline. Terminal reward state prevents a successful claim from being replayed. The typed claim has no separate nonce field; the nonce belongs to the server-side wallet challenge. This integration remains disabled without operator credentials and has not been exercised with real X accounts.

The full author journey is: resolve post and inspect author 鈫?select test ETH or tUSD and enter an optional public note 鈫?inspect the 3% claim-only fee and 90-day deadline 鈫?approve exactly the tUSD reward amount if needed and fund 鈫?share the receipt manually 鈫?author signs in with X and binds a payout wallet 鈫?claim using a configured relayer or a wallet-paid transaction 鈫?inspect received/sent history and terminal receipt. If no claim occurs, the sender can request the full amount after 90 days. Read-only public activity must show real chain records and identify synthetic engineering transactions as test activity. Test-token minting is a demo faucet, not a monetary incentive or USDG issuance.

If the verifier is unavailable, claims may be delayed. Expired unclaimed escrows remain refundable under their rules. If the verifier is compromised, it may authorize improper payouts unless additional controls intervene. Calling this architecture fully trustless would be inaccurate.

Postpaid validates that the interaction can be described simply: attach funds to a post, let its author claim, and protect the payer with expiry. Its current public documentation states a 3% claim fee and a 90-day refund period. The public homepage observed during research showed no payments; that is not evidence of commercial demand.

5. Revenue and unit economics

Commercial design

  • Consumer rewards: 3% deducted when a reward is successfully claimed. A $12 claimed reward would yield $0.36 gross fee and $11.64 to the creator, before any separately disclosed gas costs. The author testnet contract exercises this split with valueless test assets; these fees are not company revenue. The legacy wallet pilot remains fee-free.
  • Campaign software: test a $299/month Starter plan and a $499/month Team plan for budgets, reporting, access controls, and payout reconciliation. Prices are research offers, not implemented subscriptions.
  • Enterprise/API: evaluate negotiated volume pricing only after the consumer and campaign workflows operate reliably.

There is no revenue from deposit float, unclaimed funds, compulsory token purchases, trading promises, or selling users' private data. Protocol fees are recognized from successful claim events; escrow deposits are liabilities to users, not company revenue. Accounting treatment and tax reporting require a qualified accountant for the selected entity.

Acquisition discipline

At four rewards per supporter per month, base contribution is only $0.912 per active supporter-month. An assumed six active months yields $5.472 of contribution before fixed costs. This is not a measured LTV. It implies a provisional paid CAC ceiling below $1.50, making broad paid acquisition unattractive at the start. Creator distribution, referrals from real receipts, and B2B acquisition are more plausible experiments.

Do not pay people merely to claim rewards and then count those claims as organic demand. Track subsidy-funded and unsubsidized behavior separately.

Proposed operating targets

For an initial real-asset cohort, aim for at least 70% successful claims within seven days, at least 20% of first-time supporters funding again within 30 days, and positive contribution after direct costs. These are decision thresholds, not industry benchmarks. Segment by cohort size and confidence; do not infer product-market fit from a few high-value supporters.

Stop or revise the current acquisition approach if two successive matured cohorts have under 10% repeat support without incentives. Revisit the product if claim rate stays below 50% despite clear notifications and working identity, or if API and support cost consistently exceed realized fee contribution.

7. Technical architecture and trust

Pilot architecture

The Vue app communicates with an injected EVM wallet for authorization and a Robinhood testnet RPC for reads. The wallet submits native test-ETH escrow transactions. The deployed contract enforces the designated recipient, payer, amount, state, and expiry rules. Explorer links and readbacks provide independent evidence of transaction state.

There is no need to place a deployment private key in a browser bundle. The user-supplied deployment key is for local deployment tooling only. Keep it out of source control, logs, screenshots, public artifacts, and frontend environment variables. Publish only public addresses, deployment metadata, transaction hashes, ABI, and source needed to verify the deployment.

The wallet contract and separately deployed optional X-ID contract have different trust and authorization models. The X testnet contract is 0xfa0095Af3f8129710E803CE5c9855A4d1db1633F; its attestor is immutable and can authorize any active reward. It has no signer rotation or emergency pause. A compromised or lost key therefore requires a new deployment for future rewards, while existing funds retain their original rules. Production needs an explicitly reviewed key-management and migration design.

Security acceptance cases

The smart-contract suite must test unauthorized claims, unauthorized refunds, double claims, double refunds, empty amounts, invalid recipient, expiry boundaries, recipient contracts that reject ETH, and reentrancy attempts. Accounting should ensure terminal payouts cannot exceed funded value. A reverting recipient must not block unrelated escrows. Chain IDs and verifying contract addresses must be part of any future signature domain.

The OAuth release must test mismatched author identity, handle changes, reused OAuth state, invalid PKCE, expired sessions, tampered wallet binding, signature replay, stale authorization, cross-chain replay, and a disabled signer. Access-token and secret values must never appear in browser-visible errors or analytics.

The current pilot remains unaudited experimental software even when tests pass. Tests demonstrate specified behavior; they do not replace a professional audit or secure operations.

9. Legal, privacy, and asset policy

The initial testnet experience uses assets with no monetary value. A commercial launch requires choosing the operating entity, target jurisdictions, customer eligibility, asset set, and custody/verification model. Obtain counsel on payments or money-transmission rules, AML/sanctions obligations, consumer disclosures, tax reporting, advertising, and any treatment of sponsored creator rewards. A smart contract does not automatically remove regulatory obligations.

Do not promise worldwide access. Region availability should follow actual legal and operational decisions. Payment functionality should not be advertised as bank deposits, insured balances, guaranteed income, or investment returns.

Publish terms explaining fees, refund timing, irrevocable claims, identity-verifier trust, asset/network risk, support, prohibited conduct, and dispute limits. A privacy notice should explain how wallet addresses, public posts, X identifiers, access tokens, analytics, and logs are processed. Public chain data cannot be deleted from the chain; say so before users submit public notes or sensitive metadata.

Store only the minimum X data needed to resolve and verify claims, follow X's developer agreement and deletion/display requirements, and give users a revocation route. Do not scrape or rely on undocumented embeds as the sole production identity authority just because the competitor describes that approach.

Voxaura is independent of X Corp., Robinhood, and the researched projects. Using Robinhood Chain identifies a network choice, not a partnership or endorsement. Brand and trademark review remains necessary; a current domain or handle vacancy cannot prove a name has never been used anywhere.
